    From observed traffic to this model · footage & sources

    Frame sequences from two elevated-view Chennai clips were reviewed: medium-flow footage and congested footage, accompanying Kanagaraj & Treiber (2018). These are real observations; the paper’s separate simulated clips were not treated as footage of real traffic.

    Visible patternSimulation rule
    Cars and bikes follow in staggered positions.Continuous lateral positions; braking depends on longitudinal gap, closing speed and lateral overlap.
    Two-wheelers pass between wider vehicles.Different physical widths and headways; drivers search for usable gaps, with swept-body collision checks.
    Dense traffic forms irregular clusters.Shared irregular arrivals, varied desired speeds, finite acceleration and strongest-leader interaction.
    Small sideways movements often replace full lane changes.Gradual steering toward a nearby usable path. Parked vehicles remove only the width they actually occupy.

    Model: Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) acceleration with the Mixed Traffic Flow Model’s lateral attenuation idea, plus a simplified local gap-search controller. This is not an exact MTM implementation or a fitted reconstruction. Passenger dwell events and protruding parking are illustrative additions requested for this street; their frequency was not measured from these clips.

    Both roads use the same vehicle dimensions, acceleration, braking, headway parameters and offered arrivals. Only road-space management and lateral positioning differ. No target throughput ratio is imposed. See the model notes for equations, assumptions and verification.

    Outflow (PCU/min): sum of passenger-car equivalents leaving both ends of the road over the last 60 simulated seconds. During the first minute, the shorter observed window is scaled to a per-minute rate and labelled “Building window.” This measures discharge, not inflow minus outflow. Fixed project weights: car 1.0, motorcycle/scooter 0.75, auto 2.0, delivery van 2.0, bus 3.7. These are illustrative equivalents; real PCUs depend on traffic conditions. Pause freezes the measurement; 2× playback still uses simulated seconds.

    Sliders: V is offered two-way PCU/hour. Each arrival spends its actual PCU weight from a shared demand budget; the small remainder waits for the next whole vehicles. Higher violation intensity adds upper-road parked vehicles, extra car/auto/loading stops, and drivers seeking smaller rear gaps. Scheduled bus frequency and dwell remain unchanged. Actual arrivals, vehicle mix and PCUs stay identical. Changing a slider restarts both scenes with a 25-second pre-run and clears counters.

    Simulation V/C: V is selected demand, not the measured discharge. C is an approximate discharge envelope measured from this very simulation: 120 s warm-up then 480 s measurement at five demand levels, taking maximum observed discharge. At 100% violations, the estimates are 2,563 PCU/h above and 8,175 PCU/h below. Intermediate intensity estimates interpolate benchmark samples; they are not forced to decrease monotonically. This one-seed estimate is not a real-road capacity or a hard outflow cap. node traffic_flow_capacity_audit.js reproduces the benchmark. The different 3,996/5,143 PCU/h corridor-model values remain documented separately; they no longer label this wider, unsignalised animation.

    Why contrast depends on demand: at light demand both roads can clear their arrivals, so a large difference is not expected. The 8,000 PCU/h default lies between the benchmark capacities at 100% violations. Run for several simulated minutes (or use +5 simulated min) to see queues develop. At extreme demand both roads may congest. Offered PCUs, admitted PCUs and outflow are distinct: queued demand is never discarded.

    Travel times: timing starts when a vehicle’s centre crosses the entrance and ends at the 125 m exit. Pre-run entrants are excluded. The mean of completed trips is multiplied by 80, 96 or 120 for 10, 12 or 15 km. This is a repeated-conditions extrapolation, not a calibrated Bengaluru route forecast: it does not add junctions, route variation or waiting outside the entrance. “End simulation” freezes the run and shows a final comparison; Restart or changing a demand/violation slider begins a new run.
